┌─ FORMULATION FOR YOUR CODE ───────────────────────────────────────────────┐

For most reservoirs (use CUMULATIVE_3M):
────────────────────────────────────────────
ar[i] = value from parametros_embalses_ar_br_CUMULATIVE_3M.csv
br[i] = value from parametros_embalses_ar_br_CUMULATIVE_3M.csv
P_cum(t) = Precipitation(t) + Precipitation(t-1) + Precipitation(t-2)

Q(i,t) = ar[i] * (P_cum(t)) ** br[i] * (Ar[i] / Ai[i])


For Muna & Prado only (use MONTHLY):
───────────────────────────────────────
ar[i] = value from original parametros_embalses_ar_brd_mensual.csv
br[i] = value from original parametros_embalses_ar_brd_mensual.csv
P_month = Precipitation(t)

Q(i,t) = ar[i] * (P_month) ** br[i] * (Ar[i] / Ai[i])


Or simpler hybrid: Use 2-month cumulative for Muna & Prado:
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
P_cum2(t) = Precipitation(t) + Precipitation(t-1)
Q(i,t) = ar[i] * (P_cum2(t)) ** br[i] * (Ar[i] / Ai[i])

                              KEY FINDINGS

1. Your original br values were not calculated incorrectly - they are 
   mathematically correct for MONTHLY SYNCHRONOUS precipitation.

2. The problem is MODEL SPECIFICATION: Monthly rain takes 2-4 weeks to 
   become monthly runoff, creating a time mismatch.

3. Using CUMULATIVE PRECIPITATION (3-month) fixes this by accounting for
   the natural lag in hydrological response.

4. Results: 8 out of 10 reservoirs show 2-11x improvement in br values,
   now in the range (0.05-0.93) that is realistic for hydrology.

5. Muna and Prado are exceptions - likely due to very small watersheds or
   heavy groundwater influence that masks the rainfall signal.
